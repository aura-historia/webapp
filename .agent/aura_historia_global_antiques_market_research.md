# Aura Historia — Global Antiques Market Research

**Prepared:** 2026-06-21  
**Purpose:** Evidence bank for the Aura Historia landing page and marketing agent.  
**Product thesis:** The antiques, decorative arts, collectibles, and auction ecosystem is large, global, and heavily fragmented across dealers, auction houses, marketplaces, fairs, and local shops. Aura Historia can credibly position itself as a discovery and unification layer for collectors who do not want rare pieces to remain hidden in disconnected inventories.

## 3. Narrative themes for landing-page copy

### A. The market is large, global, and still active

The most credible global benchmark is the Art Basel and UBS annual art-market report. It estimates global art and antiques sales at **USD 59.6B in 2025**, up 4% year over year, across **41.5M transactions**. Dealer sales made up the largest channel at **USD 34.8B**, followed by public auction sales of **USD 20.7B** and private auction sales of just under **USD 4.2B**.

**Landing-page implication:** Aura Historia can credibly say it serves a market with tens of billions in annual sales and tens of millions of transactions — while avoiding the inaccurate claim that antiques alone are a USD 59.6B category.

**Suggested copy:**

> “A USD 59.6B art-and-antiques market still depends on fragmented discovery across dealers, auctions, fairs, and private inventories.”

### B. Fragmentation is a measurable problem

US antique stores are a **USD 4.5B** market in 2026, with **13,362 businesses** in 2024. IBISWorld explicitly describes the industry as fragmented, with no company holding more than **5%** market share. The US online antiques and collectibles sector is also made up of thousands of businesses — **10,984** in 2025 — and IBISWorld reports low concentration in that market as well.

Europe adds another long-tail seller base. IBISWorld estimates **81,256** second-hand goods retailing businesses in Europe in 2025 and **EUR 11.2B** in 2026 market size. This category is broader than antiques, but it is useful context because second-hand retailing includes antique-adjacent dealers and local sellers.

**Landing-page implication:** Do not just say “fragmented.” Use numbers: thousands of US antique stores, thousands of US online antiques and collectibles sellers, and tens of thousands of European second-hand/antique-adjacent businesses.

**Suggested copy:**

> “Thousands of antique dealers and auction houses hold inventory that never reaches the collector who is searching for it.”

### C. Small dealers and lower-price segments are where discovery is happening

The 2025 Art Basel/UBS report found that the smallest dealers — those with turnover below **USD 250k** — grew **17%** in 2024, while the largest dealers above **USD 10M** declined **9%**. Dealers also reported that **44%** of buyers were new to their businesses in 2024. For the smallest dealers, **50%** of sales were to first-time buyers.

At public auction, the under-**USD 5k** segment grew **7%** in value and **13%** in lots sold in 2024. Lots below **USD 50k** grew **8%**. This is especially useful for Aura Historia because it supports the “bargains, overlooked pieces, and collection-completing finds” story.

**Landing-page implication:** The top of the market gets attention, but growth and discovery are visible in smaller sellers and more accessible price segments.

**Suggested copy:**

> “The next piece in your collection may not be at the biggest auction house. It may be sitting with a specialist dealer no algorithm has shown you yet.”

### D. Online is significant, but not solved

Online art and antiques sales reached **USD 10.5B in 2024** and remained **76% above 2019** levels. Dealers made **22%** of sales online in 2024, compared with **13%** in 2019. Dealer-owned websites and direct channels have more than doubled since 2019, which suggests that inventory has moved online — but into many separate destinations rather than one unified discovery layer.

Apollo’s analysis of the 2026 Hiscox Online Art Trade Report estimated online art and antiques sales at about **USD 9.2B in 2025**, around **15%** of the total art market. It also emphasized that the online market has matured and become more hybrid, with buyers still relying on trust, viewing, relationships, and physical context for many purchases.

**Landing-page implication:** Aura Historia should not frame online as “new.” The stronger message is: online is established, but discovery is still fragmented and trust still matters.

**Suggested copy:**

> “Antiques have moved online. Discovery has not caught up.”

### E. Cross-border opportunity matters for a Germany-based platform

The Art Basel/UBS report recorded cross-border art and antiques trade flows of **USD 30.7B in imports** and **USD 33.4B in exports** in 2022. The 2025 market was highly international, with the US, UK, and China together representing **76%** of global sales value. For a Germany-based platform, this supports building for EU compliance and international reach from day one.

**Landing-page implication:** The platform should emphasize EU trust, multilingual/international discovery, and worldwide reach rather than a purely local German marketplace.

**Suggested copy:**

> “From European dealers to American collectors, Aura Historia is built for a market where the right buyer and the right piece are rarely in the same place.”

### F. Collector demand remains resilient

Art Basel/UBS reported that **40%** of surveyed high-net-worth collectors planned to buy more art over the next 12 months and **84%** were optimistic about the art market’s performance. The Global Collecting 2025 survey also found that HNW collectors allocated an average **20%** of their wealth to art collections in 2025, up from **15%** in 2024.

**Landing-page implication:** The landing page can speak to committed collectors who are not casually browsing; they have high intent, specific tastes, and a willingness to search globally for the right object.

**Suggested copy:**

> “Collectors are still buying. The challenge is finding the object before it disappears into another silo.”

## 8. One-paragraph landing-page research summary

The global art-and-antiques market is large, international, and fragmented: Art Basel/UBS estimated **USD 59.6B** in 2025 sales across **41.5M** transactions, while US antique stores alone represent a **USD 4.5B** market with more than **13,000** businesses and no company above **5%** share. Online behavior is now established — online art-and-antiques sales reached **USD 10.5B** in 2024 and remained far above pre-pandemic levels — but the supply side is still split across dealer websites, auction platforms, local shops, fairs, and marketplaces. The strongest discovery story sits in the long tail: the smallest dealer segment grew **17%** in 2024, dealers reported **44%** of buyers were new, and the smallest dealers generated **50%** of sales from first-time buyers. Aura Historia can therefore position itself as a global discovery layer for rare objects that would otherwise remain hidden in disconnected inventories.

## 9. Implementation notes for the landing-page agent

- Always write **“art and antiques”** when using Art Basel/UBS global market-size numbers.
- Use **“antiques and collectibles”** when using IBISWorld online US data.
- Use **“second-hand and antique-adjacent sellers”** when using the European second-hand goods retailing data.
- Prefer “fragmented,” “long-tail,” “undiscovered,” “scattered,” and “hidden inventory” over exaggerated claims like “impossible to find.”
- Pair large market-size stats with emotional collector language: “the one piece that completes a collection,” “a specialist dealer no one has shown you,” “rare objects trapped in disconnected inventories.”
- For Germany/EU trust positioning, emphasize EU-based operation, cross-border discovery, and compliance/trust rather than limiting the story to Germany.
